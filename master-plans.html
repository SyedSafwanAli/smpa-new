What a Master Plan Is

Urban Planning (town planning, city & regional planning) refers to design and regulate the uses of land/space that can impact the physical development, social behavior, cultural norms, economic growth, environment and various other aspects of urban environment.

The definition of “Master Plan”

“Master Plan” means the traditional method for presenting a set of zones, land-uses, allocations and control measures in the form of a map or layout plan or in graphical form and is supported by written statement or report of goals, objectives, existing conditions, surveys, analyses, strategies, financial implications, recommendations, framework, and policies for planning and development for an area. It includes a Master Plan, Development plan, Strategic Plan, Zonal Plan, Structure Plan, Land-Use Plan, an outline development plan, a spatial plan, peri-urban structure plan, neighborhood development plan a metropolitan plan, road network plan, contingency plans, or any other similar plan of any area, city, district, division and/or region.

In ideal scenarios, except the case of emergency, urban land is never used, allotted, auctioned, and leased without designating a suitable land-use after carrying out studies, surveys and/or other logical exercise. The Master Plans or other similar plans, prepared to guide development of an area, provide foundation with respect to many aspects, including (but not limited to):

  • Better administration, decision making and good governance.
  • Sustainable, integrated and synchronized development and redevelopment.
  • Development control, density control, building control, preservation of environment.
  • Preparation & amendment of regulations.
  • Preparation & amendments of town planning, architectural, construction, services & utility standards.
